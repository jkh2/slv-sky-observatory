### 3.3 Can a nightly 8:15 appearance from August or October be a star or planet?

A star returns to the same azimuth about **3 minutes 56 seconds earlier each solar day**. That is a fixed geometric rate, not a special assumption.

- Early August (1 August) → 21 November: **112 days ≈ 7.3 hours** earlier.  
- Early October (1 October) → 21 November: **51 days ≈ 3.3 hours** earlier.

A star seen at 20:15 in early August would reach that same sky position around **13:00 MST** in late November—daytime. A star seen at 20:15 in early October would reach it around **16:55 MST** on 21 November—near sunset, not 20:15. **A fixed star cannot stay at 8:00–8:15 p.m. from August or October through 21 November.**

Computed positions of the same objects at **20:15 MST** on earlier dates (same calculation point):

- **Venus** was a brilliant evening object in the west in **early August** (1 Aug: az 264°, alt 11°, about mag −4.3). By **1 October** it was already **20° below** the horizon at 20:15, and on **21 November** it was far below the horizon (alt −57°). Venus can explain a bright western light near 8:15 in early August; it **cannot** explain the 21 November 20:15 western field, nor a continuous 8:15 watch into late fall.  
- **Altair, Vega, and Deneb** were in the western half of the sky at 20:15 by October–November, but their altitudes and azimuths **change by tens of degrees** over those months (e.g. Altair 1 Oct alt 58° vs 21 Nov alt 24°).  
- **Mars and Jupiter** were not western evening objects at 20:15 on these dates. **Saturn** was in the south by November, not the west.

**Conclusion (derived):** commenters’ “every night at 8:15 from August or October” is **not consistent with one star or one planet**. If the recurrence is accurate, a **clock-time** source (local lights, a habitual human activity, or mixed misidentifications at a habitual watching hour) is a better class of explanation than a sidereal or single-planet source. The recurrence itself remains **unverified**.

## 5. What is known and unknown

**Known with relatively high confidence (computed or archived):**

- It was fully dark by 20:15 MST; sunset was 16:44 MST.  
- Skies at KALS were clear, 10 SM visibility.  
- The Moon was bright and in the southeast, not the west.  
- No planet stood in the western sky at 20:15.  
- Altair, Vega, and Deneb were in the west, well above the mountain horizon.  
- A star cannot hold a fixed 8:15 p.m. slot from August or October to 21 November.  
- Venus was a bright western evening object at 20:15 in early August, but not in October or on 21 November.  
- A filtered adsb.lol globe_history extract for 20:00–20:30 MST shows 21 aircraft in the SLV box, including eight in the western sky during ~20:05–20:25 MST, four of which passed within ~1.2° of the Loaf 'N Jug bearing from an approximate east-of-town observer (see §3.6.1).

**Unknown or unverified:**

- Exact observer position, azimuth, and elevation angle.  
- Video metadata (start time, duration of each clip, timezone, whether the file is original, compiled, or compressed).  
- What the flagged ~0:44 segment actually shows under an independent, preferably stabilized, frame-by-frame review. Because the file is a multi-clip compilation, 0:44 is not a time of night.  
- Whether lights strobed, and their angular size and separation.  
- Which, if any, of the documented overflights corresponds to the flagged 0:44 segment (clock time still unknown). Other non-ADS-B aircraft could also have been present.  
- Police confirmation, MUFON case number, and the “every night” / multi-site claims (see also §7).  
- Navajo Nation reports (possibly unrelated; see §7).

## 6. Questions for observers (data still needed)

These items would strengthen or falsify the hypotheses above. **Do not collect exact private addresses or names for a public file.** Approximate public place names and a compass or map-based bearing are enough.

1. **Original, unedited video file(s)** with embedded metadata (creation time, timezone, device, any zoom or stabilization). Prefer the camera-original, not a Facebook recompress or a compiled highlight reel.  
2. **Independent frame-by-frame review of the ~0:44 segment** of the 1:48 compilation, including a version **stabilized against the horizon and town lights**. Do background lights stay fixed while any other light moves, or do all lights move together?  
3. **Precise time of night** for that segment in the **original** (uncompiled) file, so it can be matched against flight and satellite tracks. A timestamp of 0:44 in a multi-clip compilation **cannot** supply that clock time.  
4. **Approximate bearing and elevation** for each distinct light (horizon red light vs any higher points): even “a fist above the mountain line, toward the town lights” is useful. A compass app or a still with the horizon labeled would help.  
5. Did the **original witness** see motion **with the unaided eye**, or only later on the recording?  
6. **Color and blink pattern** over the 5–10 minute recording window (steady, flashing, red/green/white, strobe).  
7. **Sound**, if any (drone buzz, rotor, jet).  
8. Were the lights **over the valley floor / town**, or **above the mountain band**? Did they pass **in front of or behind** any known landmark?  
9. For recurrence claims (only if the commenter consents to a public, non-identifying note): first date, whether it was truly nightly, and whether the same *direction and height* were involved.  
10. Any **MUFON case number** or **public** police statement (no private contact details).
